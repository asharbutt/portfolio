Multidimensionality

For more involved stochastic models such as using stochastic volatility, we need to look into multidimensional stochastic processes. We start with defining and working through correlation for multiple brownian motions, which we can use to connect systems of SDEs. We start with the Cholesky Decomposition of the correlation matrix:

Cholesky Decomposition

We know that for any two independent Brownian motions $W(t_{1}), W(t_{2})$, their covariance can be defined as $E[W_{i}W_{j}]=t$. This is a result directly from taking a single dimension Brownian motion, and treating each outcome of the path as a variable, and then taking the covariance of two samples: $E[W(t)W(s)]= s\land t$. We know that two Brownian motions are independent if their covariances are 0. Similarly, if two Brownian motions are correlated, then their covariance will be: $E[W_{i}W_{j}]=\rho _{i,j}t$
