Binomial Model

Under the Binomial process, the underlying follows a Binomial model. In this case, the underlying asset S, at every time step has two possible outcomes. We start off by assuming that the asset has a value of $S_{0}$ at $t_{0}$, and for every time step has two potential outcomes of $uS_{0}$ and $dS_{0}$ with a probability $p_{u}$ and $p_{d}$. We construct a replicating portfolio made up of $\delta$ amounts of S and a risk-less bond with a notional value of n, which grows at a rate of $e^{rT}$. This means that at the end of the time step, the value of the portfolio will either be $C_{u} = \delta uS_{0} + ne^{r\Delta T}$ or $C_{u} = \delta dS_{0} + ne^{r\Delta T}$

We can solve these two equations to get the value of $\delta$ and n, simply by substitution:

$$\delta uS_{0} - C_{u} = ne^{r\Delta T}$$

$$\delta dS_{0} - C_{d} = ne^{r\Delta T}$$

Therfore:

$$\delta dS_{0} - C_{d} = \delta uS_{0} - C_{u}$$

$$\delta = \frac{C_{u}-C_{d}}{(u-d)S_{0}}$$

We do the same for n to get: $\frac{uC_{d}-dC_{u}}{(u-d)e^{r\Delta T}}$

Because this is a replicating portfolio, we expect that the value of the option at the start, V(0), is equal to the value of the portfolio at the start: $V(0) = \delta S_{0} + n$. Because the bond is a riskless bond, it is worth 1 at the start, and grows at the bank account rate. We can then substitute in our values derived above, such that:

$$V(0)=\delta S_{0}+n=S_{0}\frac{C_{u}-C_{d}}{(u-d)S_{0}} + \frac{uC_{d}-dC_{u}}{(u-d)e^{r\Delta T}}$$

$$V(0)=\frac{C_{u}-C_{d}}{(u-d)} + \frac{uC_{d}-dC_{u}}{(u-d)e^{r\Delta T}}$$

$$V(0)=e^{-r\Delta T}(\frac{e^{r\Delta T}C_{u}-e^{r\Delta T}C_{d}}{(u-d)} + \frac{uC_{d}-dC_{u}}{(u-d)})$$

We rearrange to collect the up and down option values on either side:

$$V(0)=e^{-r\Delta T}(\frac{e^{r\Delta T}C_{u}-dC_{u}}{(u-d)} + \frac{uC_{d}e^{r\Delta T}C_{d}}{(u-d)})$$

$$V(0)=e^{-r\Delta T}(\frac{e^{r\Delta T}-d}{(u-d)}C_{u} + \frac{u-e^{r\Delta T}}{(u-d)}C_{d})$$

If we then substitute the variable: $p=\frac{e^{r\Delta T}-d}{u-d}$, then we will get:

$$V(0)=e^{-r\Delta T}(pC_{u} + (1-p)C_{d})$$

We can consider p as the probability here under a certain measure, meaning that \(0 < p < 1\), and in order for that to hold, \(d < e^{r\Delta T} < u\)

Given that under the risk neutral measure, $E^{Q}[S_{\Delta T}\mvert F_{t}]=S_{t}e^{r\Delta T}$. Given that under the Binomial process, the expected value is given by $puS_{0} + (1-p)dS_{0}$, we know that p is the probability of an up move under the risk neutral measure, and p is the associated probability measure. We first compute the payoffs at maturity, then work our way back using the above equation for option prices. WE discount until we get back to t=0.
