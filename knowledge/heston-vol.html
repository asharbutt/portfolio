Heston Volatility

Introduction

Black Scholes model assumes that the volatility is constant across all strikes and maturities. The implied volatilities from the market say otherwise, and show that there is a skew present. The Local Volatility model is great for vanilla options as it is able to replicate and reproduce the market implied surface perfectly, given it is just a transformation of the surface. However, if we are to simulate some option prices to the future, and then imply the volatility back towards another point in the future (such as a forward start option), we note that the implied skew is actually much flatter than what we would expect. This is a limitation of the local vol mdoel, and thus it is not a good vol model to use for products which rely on forward skews.

We therefore introduce a stochastic volatility model under which the variance process itself is stochastic. We start of with the Schobel-Zhu process, which is the adapted Ohrnstein-Uhlenbeck process from interest rates:

$$\boxed{\begin{cases} dS(t) = rS(t)dt + \sigma S(t) dW \\ d\sigma (t) = \kappa (\={\sigma} - \sigma)dt + \gamma dW \end{cases}}$$
