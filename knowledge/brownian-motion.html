Brownian Motion

A Brownian Motion is a random variable W(t) defined on the probability space $(\Omega, F, P)$ which fulfils the following conditions:

  • 1. W(0)=0
  • 2. W(t) is a martingale i.e. E[W(t)$\vert$F(s)] = W(s)
  • 3. The increments of a Brownian Motion are normally distributed with a mean 0 and a variance equal to the difference in time.

The Brownian Motion also has quadratic variance at a rate of the change of time. Unlike smooth continious functions where you can zoom in enough to be able to describe the change as a linear change, a Brownian Motion can be zoomed into infinitely i.e. the function is not differentiable anywhere.

Markov Property

Let W(t) be a Brownian Motion on the probability space $(\Omega, F, P)$. Then W(t) is a Markov process. This means that the transition density of W(t) only depends on the value of W(s) for s $\leq$t, and the mean for the transition density will then be W(s), i.e. the only value that is relevant to the transition of W(t) is the value at the current time s, nothing before.

First Passage time and the Reflection Principle

Let W(t) be a Brownian motion with an associated filtration F on a probability space. Let T be a stopping time such that $(\omega :T(\omega )\leq t \forall T \in \Omega$. We are saying that T is a stopping time if and only if a sample or value $\omega$ is in the adapted filtration, basically if the outcome has already happened, such that the time it has occured is less than the current value of time.

Then, the first passage time principle describes the time $\tau _{m}$ as the first time the Brownian Motion associated with the filtration of the space crosses a real value m, such that:

\tau _{m} = inf(t \geq 0: W_{t}=m)

The optional sampling theorem states that for a martingale ${M_{t}:t\geq 0}$, with a bounded stopping time, $E[M_{t}]=M_{0}$. Any martingale stopped or frozen at some time t is still considered a martingale, therefore it must maintain the property of a martingale and the expected value of such martingale must be the value at time 0, or the last time its value was measured.
