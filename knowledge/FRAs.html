Forward Rate Agreement

A forward rate agreement, or FRA is a contract which allows two parties to exchange two rates at a future date. This usually involves the exchange of a fixed rate for a floating reference rate, which is decided on the fixing date. FRAs can be used to lock in and hedge interest rate exposure, allowing clients to convert exposure from floating to fixed, and vice versa.

FRAs are quoted by Start x End, for example 3 month x 6 month, meaning that in 3 months time, we observe or start the fixing of the reference rate, and then 3 months after we settle. For LIBOR, the way this works is that n days (usually 2 business days) before your FRA start, you observe the fixing. You then exchange the difference between the fixing rate and the fixed rate on the settlement date, which tends to be at the end of the reference period (3 months for a 3M-LIBOR, 6M for 6M-LIBOR). If not, compounding is usually involved.
